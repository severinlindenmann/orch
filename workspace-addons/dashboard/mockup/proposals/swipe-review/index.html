Swipe review

Everything that waits on you, one card at a time. Right = Looks good, left = Don't do, down = Comment (voice or text). The middle of the card is the thing itself: a screen, a draft, a file, a link. Tap it to make it bigger, open it, or comment on one spot.

Phone app (6 states, one live) Workspace dashboard (live, keys work)

Research: what others do, what we take

WhereWhat they learnedWe take
Tinder, Bumble (card stacks)One decision at a time is fast and calm. A stamp while dragging tells you what will happen; let go early and it springs back. Buttons under the card for people who don't swipe.One card, the stamp, three buttons, the card behind peeks out.
Apple Mail, Gmail swipe actionsSwipes fire by accident. Fix: a clear distance (about a third of the width) or a fast flick, and Undo right after.30 % width or a flick; Undo toast 5 s; Z on the desk.
Superhuman, Linear TriageOn a computer, triage is a keyboard job: one key per answer, preview with Space.← ↓ → and Space; drag still works with the mouse.
Before/after sliders (photo apps, Percy / Chromatic visual tests, GitHub image diff)A change is judged fastest next to what was there: a slider for screens, side by side for text.Slide line in the big view, Before · After · Both on the card (no slider on the card, it would fight the swipe).
Polls, A/B design reviews (Figma voting, Lyssna preference tests)Choosing is easier with all options side by side and one clear pick; comments are about one option.Options card: side by side on the desk, stacked on the phone; comment “about C”.
Photo grids, Tinder “rewind”, mail inbox listA stack hides what is left; people want to see the whole pile and go back.“All cards” overview + jump; answers can be changed.
Figma, Markup, GitHub review“Change this” is much clearer on the spot than in a sentence.“Comment on a spot” in the big view.
WhatsApp voice notes (and our messenger)Hold to talk is the quickest way to say a long thing on the phone.Hold to talk in the comment sheet, written out like the chat does.
Accessibility (WCAG 2.2: 2.5.1, 2.5.7; iOS VoiceOver)Every swipe needs a one tap way, a drag is never the only way; screen readers need named actions.Buttons always there; VoiceOver actions “Looks good / Don't do / Comment”; Reduce Motion = no fly-out.
Known risk: “rubber stamping”Fast stacks tempt you to approve without looking.Each card says what happens when you approve; risky ones still need the signature; the stack stays short (what waits now).

My recommendation

  • Put in the stack: drafts, Check results, helper plans that need a yes, links before they go public. Questions with several choices stay normal question cards (a swipe can't pick option B).
  • Left word: “Don't do”, but “Send back” on Check results (that is the word the ticket already uses).
  • Swipe up: keep it free for now (later maybe “Later” = to the end of the stack).
  • Comment = send back with your words. A “Looks good, plus a note” is a pin or comment and then swipe right.
  • Build: one widget in the master design system (already in this draft: UI.swipeDeck, used by both pages), one Review page in the dashboard, one SwiftUI screen in the app. Answers use the same paths as today's Looks good / Send back and the question answers, so nothing new on the wire.
